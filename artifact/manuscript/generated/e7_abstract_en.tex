Across 48 independently generated training datasets, no training or validation episode meets the prespecified severe-crisis criterion. In the primary scenario, where load increases rework, preparation reduces cost by 38.2\% against planning with load-independent processing times and rework and by 38.1\% against adding a worker at 20 unfinished tasks. Both adjusted paired confidence intervals exclude zero. Superiority over reducing rework at the same threshold of 20 unfinished tasks was not established. Across four mechanisms, descriptive normalized comparisons favor model-based action selection over either fixed action, but expose a loss without feedback and sensitivity to action cost.
